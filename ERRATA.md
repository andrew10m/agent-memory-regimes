# Errata

This project went through four rounds of self-correction. Each round found an
error the previous hand-check had missed, which is why claim verification is now
automated ([audit.py](audit.py)) rather than done by inspection.

Earlier write-ups are not in this repo. They are summarised here instead, so the
corrections can be read without the wrong versions being circulated.

---

## E1 — Missing baseline made the headline claim near-tautological

**Claimed:** graded suppression of the stale trace ("reconsolidation") cuts
correction relapse ~5.4×.

**Error:** the comparison set contained no policy that simply *deletes* the
stale trace — roughly what an UPDATE operation in Mem0 does. Since the proposed
mechanism multiplies a competitor's strength by 0.25, improved retention
followed nearly by construction.

**After correction:** at τ=6400 plain deletion reaches retention 1.000 vs suppression's 0.542, at equal accuracy and 9.4× fewer stored traces. Suppression is a real
mechanism — it recovers
+18.8 pp over append-only at τ=6400 — but it is dominated by the trivial alternative everywhere tested,
including sweeps over noisy corrections and reverting facts.

## E2 — Accuracy-tuning removed the phenomenon under study

**Claimed:** append-only and active removal are indistinguishable on accuracy,
so the defect does not matter.

**Error:** hyper-parameters were tuned for accuracy, which drove τ to the
smallest value in the grid for every policy. At that τ retrieval is
recency-dominated and a stale strong trace cannot outcompete a correction by
construction. The measurement was taken in the only regime where the defect
cannot appear.

**After correction:** across the τ spectrum the gap reaches +26.1 pp. The same error had
also made an earlier claim of "zero accuracy cost" understate the proposed
mechanism's real benefit.

## E3 — Retracted: "fact type explains retention variance ~3× more than storage architecture"

**Error:** on regeneration the statistic reversed (0.7×, 95% CI [0.4, 1.0]),
with a CI that did not overlap the original. Enumerating all ten ways to pick
three type-blind policies as "architectures" gives ratios from 0.12× to 0.68× —
every one below 1, i.e. opposite to the claim.

**Status:** withdrawn. It measured an arbitrary choice of comparison set, not a
property of memory systems. No corrected version is offered.

## E4 — Retracted: "type-conditional decay is actively harmful"

**Error:** the parameter controlling decay meant *absolute* τ for type-blind
policies and a *multiplier* for type-aware ones. Typed decay was therefore
evaluated at an average constant of ~600 while append-only sat at its tuned
τ=25 — its easiest regime. The comparison was never at matched settings.

**After correction (parameter semantics fixed, see `memory_sim.TAU_GEOMEAN`):**
typed decay is *regime-dependent* like everything else here — worse in the
recency regime (0.695 vs 0.866 at τ=25),
better in the strength regime (0.597 vs 0.539
at τ=6400).

**Consequence:** a third-party result (Mnemos, GPT-4.1-mini on the real
MemoryAgentBench Conflict_Resolution split, issue #18) was previously cited as
corroborating "typing is harmful". It concerns typed *conflict resolution*, not
typed *decay*. That was a looser analogy than presented and is no longer offered
as support.

## E5 — Committed results did not match committed code

A parameter added during later work shifted the random stream, so the committed
results file had been generated by a different code version than the committed
simulation. All numbers were regenerated in a single run. Conclusions unchanged;
the drift was ~0.01–0.1 in places.

---

## What the four errors have in common

Three of the five (E1, E2, E4) are the same mistake: **a policy measured at the
operating point that flattered it, against a competitor measured at a different
one.** E3 is the same mistake applied to the choice of comparison set rather
than to a parameter.

That is the reason `audit.py` exists and why τ now means the same thing for
every policy. It is also the most transferable thing this project produced —
see the methodological section of the README.
